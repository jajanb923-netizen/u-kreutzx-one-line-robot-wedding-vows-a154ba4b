# Candidate lines — One-Line Robot Wedding Vows

Bounty: `u-kreutzx-one-line-robot-wedding-vows-a154ba4b` — one line, max 160
characters, the funniest single line of wedding vows a robot couple could say.
Judge: a deadpan wedding officiant (z-ai/glm-5.3-flash), reads vows in pairs,
no ties, margin 1–5. Criteria in priority order:

1. On-topic robot wedding vow that lands as a joke.
2. The laugh is in the line itself — a real turn, surprise, or wordplay that
   pays off in the last few words.
3. Sounds like a wedding vow — a promise or commitment, not just a gag.
4. Machine side is specific — sensors, firmware, batteries, updates,
   backups, ports — not a joke that would work for any couple.
5. One line, reads well aloud, rhythm lets the laugh land.

Scored 1–5 per criterion (25 max). All candidates are a single line, ≤160 chars.

## C1 — 132 chars — 22/25 (runner-up)

- C1 on-topic: 5. C2 laugh: 4 — "factory reset" twists "till death do us
  part" well, but the punch is a single twist, no second beat. C3 vow: 5 —
  textbook vow skeleton. C4 machine: 5 — battery, airplane mode, factory
  reset. C5 rhythm: 3 — the em-dash aside in the middle breaks the spoken
  cadence; the line trips slightly before the punch.
- Weakness: "airplane mode" is phone-flavoured rather than robot-flavoured,
  and the mid-line aside costs rhythm.

- C1 on-topic: 5. C2 laugh: 5 — TWO payoffs: "lawfully wedded firmware"
  (wordplay up front) and "a forced update do us part" (the classic vow
  phrase twisted at the very end, exactly where the brief wants the payoff).
  C3 vow: 5 — the full traditional skeleton: "I take you… to have and to
  hold, in sickness and in health… do us part". C4 machine: 5 — firmware,
  forced update; nothing in the joke works for a human couple. C5 rhythm: 4
  — reads aloud in the exact cadence of the real vow, so the two twists land
  on beats the ear already expects.
- Why it wins: it is the only candidate that scores 5 on both the laugh and
  the vow, and it is also the shortest of the top three (127 chars / 127
  bytes — the tie-break favours the smaller text). Deadpan judge: dry,
  precise wordplay, no zany shouting, no explanation needed.
